If you live with ADHD or executive dysfunction, you probably know the phrase **"ADHD Tax"** by heart.

It is the quiet financial penalty of having a brain that struggles with working memory, time blindness, and administrative friction: late library fees, vegetables wilting in the crisper drawer, missed return windows, and more than almost anything else today, **forgotten app subscriptions**.

Now look at the App Store and Google Play whenever you search for *"ADHD focus app"*, *"body doubling"*, or *"calm timer"*.

Almost every top result uses the exact same playbook: a slick 7-day free trial that requires your payment details up front, followed by an auto-renewing subscription of $8, $15, or even $30 a month.

Pause and think about how cynical that is.

## Profiting from the very struggle an app claims to solve

Apps designed for people who have a hard time remembering dates, tracking recurring obligations, and navigating tedious cancellation flows should be the *last* products on earth to rely on auto-renewing trials.

Yet the subscription economy actively rewards forgetfulness. When a user tries a focus app during a burst of motivation on Sunday night, loses track of it by Wednesday, and forgets to cancel before day seven, the billing system quietly charges them, often for months, before they notice it on a bank statement.

And even when you *do* actively use the app, a subscription adds a subtle layer of background guilt:

* *I'm paying $12 a month for this, so I ought to use it every day.*
* *I skipped two weeks, and now I'm wasting money.*

Suddenly, a tool that was supposed to lower pressure carries a monthly invoice reminding you whether you "got your money's worth."

## The hidden second cost: accounts and behavioral tracking

Subscriptions rarely travel alone. Right behind them come mandatory accounts, cloud sync you never asked for, and third-party analytics SDKs.

Think about what a focus, breathing, or decision app actually sees if it phones home: when you cannot sleep at 2 a.m., when you hit sensory overload during the workday, how often you abandon a task after six minutes, and which days you barely have the energy to function.

That is deeply personal rhythm data. You should never have to hand it over to ad networks, data brokers, or cloud servers just to sit quietly beside a focus companion or do a five-minute breathing exercise.

> A digital retreat cannot feel safe if it is quietly metering your attention, logging your low-energy days, and waiting for you to forget a renewal date.

## My alternative: 100% offline, zero tracking, zero subscriptions

When I started building **neuro / dev** as a neurodivergent solo developer, I made a simple commitment that applies to every single app I ship:

* **100% offline:** Your apps work on trains, planes, and offline days, without accounts, logins, or cloud lock-in.
* **Zero tracking, zero ads:** No analytics SDKs, no ad networks, no behavioral profiles. Your focus minutes, your puzzles, your breathing sessions, and even your custom photos stay on your device.
* **No subscriptions, ever:** Either an app is completely free forever (supported by honest, voluntary *Pay-What-You-Want* appreciation), or it follows a transparent **Free-to-Try + One-Time Purchase** model.

You can see that same standard across the entire **neuro / dev** library:

* [nalua](/en/nalua) (haptic breathing) and [yurua](/en/yurua) (zero-pressure decision maker) are **100% free forever**, with zero paywalls. Anyone who wants to support development can leave a voluntary one-time tip.
* [koushi](/en/koushi) (word-fit puzzles), [abayo 2048](/en/2048) (handcrafted 2048 brainteaser), and [Calm Color Reveal](/en/calm-color-reveal) (low-stimulation coloring for kids and adults) all give you generous permanent free content, with no trials that auto-convert, and a single, transparent one-time unlock if you want the full collection.

There are no virtual coin bundles, no "Pro Passes" disguised as subscriptions, and no dark patterns nudging you at your most vulnerable moment.
